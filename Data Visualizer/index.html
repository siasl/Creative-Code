My Spotify Liked Songs

These charts visualize my Spotify Liked Songs playlist: when I added each song, what genre it is, and how it sounds.

I exported the playlist as JSON using an n8n workflow. n8n has a built-in Spotify node, so I didn't have to deal with Spotify's OAuth myself, which is a pain to work with directly.

Spotify's API no longer makes it easy to get genres, keys, tempos, or other audio features. I assume they restricted that data to fight AI scraping. So I used Claude to pull the missing metadata from ReccoBeats, MusicBrainz, Deezer, Wikidata, and Wikipedia, and then I filled in the last few gaps myself by looking up those songs.

Each chart has a different level of AI assistance. I wrote a little more than half of the first chart myself, starting from an example in the D3 documentation and using AI like a supercharged Stack Overflow: asking how to approach something conceptually or structurally rather than having it write the code. As the weekend went on, I realized I wanted to explore more kinds of visualizations than I had time to build by hand with data this rich, so some of the charts are fully vibe coded just to see what's possible.

Songs added per month

Bars show how many songs I added each month. The red line shows the running total in the playlist (right axis). It only counts songs I still have liked, since un-liked songs aren't in the export. Hover over a bar or the line for exact numbers.

Vowels in song titles

How many songs have a given number of vowels in their title. Each bar is split by vowel to show how much of those titles' vowels are a, e, i, o, or u. Hover over a slice for the exact share.

Genre universe

Inspired by Nadieh Bremer's Intangible Cultural Heritage piece. Every dot is a genre with at least 10 of my songs, grouped and colored by genre family. A line connects two genres when the same songs are tagged with both; thicker lines mean more shared songs. Hover over a genre to see what it connects to, with album covers from that genre, or click it to keep it selected. Broad labels like "rock" and "alternative" are left out because they'd connect to everything.

Civilization of genres

A Civilization VI–style map of my library. Each genre family is a civilization, and its territory has one hex tile per song, so bigger empires have more songs (families with under 20 songs are city-states). Cities are sub-genres; the biggest is the capital (★) and each city's population is its number of songs. Every tile is a song: tiles fill outward from the capital in the order I added them, so the oldest songs are at the capital and the newest are on the frontier. Terrain comes from the song's audio features (see the key below). The leader is my most-liked artist in that family.

Album cover colors

Every song, colored by the most common color in its album cover. Black dominates a third of my covers, so switch to "main accent color" to see the colors underneath. Cover color has almost no relationship to how a song sounds (its energy, mood, or danceability), but it does vary by genre: pop covers lean purple, pink, and blue, folk and country lean brown and green, and hip hop covers are rarely red. Since every song here is one I liked, this can't show whether color affects what I like, only what the songs I like look like.
